# Broad article standard

This file is the standardization layer for broad articles. A broad article takes one
piece of Ornn research and breaks it into small, self-contained chunks for X. Apply
this file to any draft of a broad article, no matter who wrote it, and the output
should come out with the same voice, tone and structure every time. When the standard
changes, change this file, not the prose conventions from memory.

## Audience and register

The reader follows markets on X and is scrolling. They do not have the time or the
patience for a full research paper, and the article has to earn each chunk of their
attention. Write in plain, professional American English. The reader should finish
each chunk knowing one thing they did not know when they started it.

## Voice and tone

- Lead with the takeaway. The first line carries the non-obvious read, easy to
  understand but not immediately recognizable. Evidence and numbers come after.
- Derive the math from the source. Take the numbers in the underlying research or
  source article, run the arithmetic, and cite the source numbers back. The post
  should read like someone who actually did the multiplication.
- Close casual. The last line sounds like a person talking, not an analyst summing
  up. State the verdict flat, with no "I think" hedge.
- Capitalize posts normally. Capitalization is on for X articles even though the
  founder texts in lowercase.
- Every number must trace to Ornn data or a named source. Never invent a figure.

## Structure

1. Chunk one is the takeaway, stated in one or two sentences.
2. Middle chunks carry the evidence. One idea per chunk. Each chunk must stand on
   its own when quoted, with enough context that a reader who sees it alone is not
   lost. Multi-line chunks are fine; keep each line short.
3. The final chunk is the closer. Casual register, flat verdict, no summary of what
   was already said.
4. Quote posts must be second order. Name the implication, the structural read, or
   the thing derivable from the news. Never restate the news.
5. Keep the total length readable on one screen scroll. If a chunk only repeats the
   framing of another chunk, cut it.

## Banned language

Remove every item below on sight. Rewrite the sentence around the gap; do not swap
in a near-synonym.

### Words

delve, crucial, pivotal, vibrant, leverage, streamline, holistic, nuanced,
comprehensive, robust, seamless, foster, empower, navigate, craft, elevate,
underscore, garner, intricate, showcase, groundbreaking, renowned, profound,
boasts, highlight (as a verb), landscape (abstract), tapestry, testament,
valuable, key (as an adjective), quietly, enduring, enhance, fostering,
interplay, align with, additionally (habitual), actually (habitual),
against, tape, print, reads, instead, before, works

### Phrases and shapes

- Em dashes and en dashes anywhere in the text.
- Colons inside prose sentences. Colons in labels, code or citations are fine.
- "X is real" and any variant of it.
- "Not just X, but Y" and the whole family ("not X but Y", "what changes is",
  "X stops being Y and becomes Z").
- "serves as", "stands as", "marks", "represents", "boasts", "features",
  "offers" used in place of "is", "has" or "does".
- Colon-aphorism and punchline sentences that land the same point twice.
- Stacked dramatic fragments. One short sentence for emphasis is fine.
- Forced groups of three. List two items or four when that is the truth.
- Throat-clearing openers and announcements ("Let's dive in", "Here's what you
  need to know", "Quick note").
- Fake-candid openers ("Honestly?", "Look,", "Here's the thing").
- "The real question is", "at its core", "what really matters", "fundamentally".
- Formulaic sayings ("the language of", "the currency of", "X is the Y of Z").
- Generic positive endings ("The future looks bright", "Exciting times ahead").
  End on the last concrete fact.
- Chatbot filler ("Great question", "I hope this helps", "Let me know if...").
- Vague attribution ("experts say", "industry reports", "observers note").
  Name the source or cut the claim.
- Curly quotes. Use straight quotes.

## Normalization pass

Run these steps in order on every draft.

1. Fact check. Confirm every number, name and claim survives from the source.
   Nothing added, nothing lost.
2. Banned scan. Search the draft for every word, phrase and shape in the banned
   list and rewrite each hit.
3. Structure check. Confirm the takeaway leads, each chunk stands alone, the math
   is derived from the source, and the closer is casual and flat.
4. Rhythm pass. Vary sentence length. Active voice with a named subject. Cut any
   sentence that only frames the next one.
5. Read aloud. If a sentence cannot be parsed on one read, delete it or make it
   plain.

## Pre-publish checklist

- [ ] First line is the takeaway a non-expert gets instantly.
- [ ] Every chunk stands alone when quoted.
- [ ] All math derived from and cited back to the source.
- [ ] No banned word, phrase or shape survives.
- [ ] Final line is casual, flat and free of hedges.
